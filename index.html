Big Unit 1 · Weeks 1–8

Cell Biology & Life Processes

Students build the foundational science-process skills (experimental design, variables, graphing, CER) that anchor the rest of the course, then apply them to cell structure, membrane transport, homeostasis, and how cells capture and use energy.

⏱ 8 weeks 📋 4 labs 📝 1 checkpoint quiz + 1 unit test
Essential Questions
  • What are the defining structures and processes that make a cell the basic unit of life?
  • How do cells regulate what enters and exits, and why does this matter for survival?
  • How do cells capture and use energy to power life's processes?

Big Unit 2 · Weeks 9–12

Ecology & Populations

Students zoom out from the cell to the ecosystem, tracing energy flow through food webs and trophic levels, then examining what drives population growth, competition, and community structure.

⏱ 4 weeks 📋 2 labs 📝 1 quiz + 1 unit test
Essential Questions
  • How does energy flow and matter cycle through an ecosystem?
  • What determines whether a population grows, stabilizes, or declines?
  • How are organisms interconnected within their community and environment?

Big Unit 3 · Weeks 13–17

Evolution & Biodiversity

Students examine the evidence for evolution and the mechanism of natural selection, then apply classification systems and evaluate human impact on biodiversity — culminating in an independent research project styled as a practice IA.

⏱ 5 weeks (incl. final review) 📋 1 lab + 1 project 📝 1 unit test + final exam
Essential Questions
  • What evidence supports the theory of evolution by natural selection?
  • How does natural selection produce the diversity of life we see today?
  • How do human activities affect biodiversity, and what can be done about it?
