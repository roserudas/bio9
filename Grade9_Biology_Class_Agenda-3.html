Reaction Time Ruler Drop — Fair Test Report

Name:Partner(s):Date:Period:
How it works: Your partner holds a 30 cm ruler vertically with the 0 cm mark level with the top of your open thumb and finger. Without warning, they drop it. Catch it as fast as you can and read the distance (cm) at the top of your thumb. Use the conversion table to turn distance into reaction time.

1 · Research Question

Does   affect a person's reaction time?

Ideas: dominant vs. non-dominant hand · eyes open vs. a sound cue · before vs. after 30 s of exercise · with vs. without distraction (counting backward).

2 · Variables

Independent variable (what I change)
Dependent variable (what I measure)
Controlled variable 1 (and how)
Controlled variable 2 (and how)
Controlled variable 3 (and how)

3 · Hypothesis

Use the form: If … then … because …

4 · Method

  1. Catcher sits with forearm resting on the table edge, hand open ~2 cm.
  2. Dropper lines the 0 cm mark with the top of the catcher's thumb and drops at a random moment.
  3. Record the catch distance to the nearest 0.5 cm. If the ruler is missed, repeat that trial.
  4. Complete 5 trials for each condition of the independent variable.

7 · CER Conclusion

Claim — Answer the research question in one sentence.

Evidence — Quote specific numbers from your data (means, differences).

Reasoning — Explain the biology (nervous system: eyes → brain → muscles) and why the data support your claim.

Key Ideas & Formulas

  • Total magnification = eyepiece magnification × objective magnification
  • 1 mm = 1000 µm (micrometres)
  • Estimated cell length = field-of-view diameter ÷ number of cells that fit across it
  • Magnification = image size ÷ actual size  →  Actual size = image size ÷ magnification (same units!)

Part A · Measure the Field of View (FOV)

Place a clear ruler under the lowest objective and count the millimetre spaces across the circle. Higher-power FOVs are too small to measure directly, so calculate them:

FOVhigh = FOVlow × (magnificationlow ÷ magnificationhigh)

Exponential growth (J-curve)
Resources are unlimited, so the population grows faster and faster — it keeps multiplying by the same factor.
Logistic growth (S-curve)
Growth slows as resources run low and levels off at the carrying capacity (K) — the largest population the environment can support.

Practice IA — Independent Research Project Guide

Name:Teacher check-in:Due:
In IB DP Biology, the Internal Assessment (IA) is an independent scientific investigation that counts toward your final grade. This "practice IA" uses the same four-criterion structure in a smaller, Grade 9 version so you know what to expect. Your write-up should be about 3–6 pages including tables and graphs.

Timeline

DayGoalTeacher sign-off
Day 11Choose topic; write a focused research question; plan variables and method; risk assessment
Day 12Collect data (minimum 5 IV values × 3 trials, or an approved data-base set)
Day 13Process data: means, % change, graph with title/units; begin conclusion
Day 14Finish CER conclusion and evaluation; self-assess with the rubric; submit

Step 1 · Pick a Topic

Ideas that connect to this course (or propose your own):

  • Effect of light intensity or color on Elodea bubble rate
  • Effect of sugar concentration on yeast CO₂ production (balloon method)
  • Effect of salt concentration on % mass change of potato or carrot
  • Effect of temperature on enzyme activity (catalase in liver/potato + H₂O₂)
  • Effect of caffeine or exercise on reaction time (ruler drop)
  • Effect of distance from a path on plant species density (quadrats)
  • Database: relationship between habitat loss and species decline (published data)

Step 2 · Research Question

Format: How does [IV, with range and units] affect [DV, with units] in [organism/system]?

Independent variable (5+ values)
Dependent variable (how measured)
Controlled variables (3+, and how)
Background (why it matters, the biology)
Safety, ethical, environmental notes

Step 3 · Write-Up Structure

  1. Introduction — research question, background biology, hypothesis with a reason
  2. Method — materials and numbered steps clear enough for someone else to repeat; variables table
  3. Results — raw data table (units, uncertainties), processed data table, one correctly drawn graph
  4. Conclusion — CER: answer the research question using specific data; explain the biology
  5. Evaluation — strengths, weaknesses/sources of error, realistic improvements, extensions

Step 4 · Rubric (modeled on the DP Biology IA criteria)

ConclusionConclusion stated but not linked to dataConclusion supported by some data; biology partly explainedConclusion fully supported by specific data and explained with correct biology
